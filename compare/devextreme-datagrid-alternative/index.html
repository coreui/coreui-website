DevExtreme DataGrid Alternatives: 8 Data Grids Compared (2026)

DevExtreme DataGrid is the flagship component of DevExpress’s JavaScript suite, and for classic line-of-business applications (forms, validation, master-detail, grouped totals) it is one of the most complete grids you can buy. Teams look for an alternative for three reasons that have little to do with feature count: the grid is sold only as part of a suite subscription ($899.99 per developer per year for DevExtreme Complete); the licence is not perpetual in the usual sense (after expiry you may keep the last version you received, but a licence-verification message appears in the browser console); and the grid has no cell range selection and no clipboard copy at all, which surprises every team whose users live in Excel.

This page compares DevExtreme with seven alternatives on features, framework coverage, three-year cost, and migration effort. CoreUI publishes this page and its own Data Grid appears below, placed where it belongs rather than first.

How we compared. Published documentation and pricing as of August 2026, re-checked in October 2026. Claims that attack a vendor were reproduced first; the DevExtreme clipboard claim was tested in the official v26.1.4 demo. Performance slogans were ignored.

What DevExtreme does well, and who should stay on it

Credit first, because there is a lot of it. DevExtreme DataGrid has five editing modes (row, cell, batch, form, popup) with validation in all of them; four layers of filtering including an AND/OR filter builder; band columns; master-detail; group and total summaries with custom aggregates; and stateStoring. Server-side work is first class: remoteOperations hands filtering, paging, sorting, grouping, and summaries to the server, and DevExtreme.AspNet.Data implements that contract for ASP.NET. Localization ships 36 dictionaries plus RTL, and 26.1 added an AI assistant on the grid itself.

The commercial terms are fairer than the “suite pricing” label suggests: $899.99 per developer per year covers Angular, React, Vue, and jQuery, includes full source code, renews at exactly 50% ($449.99), has volume discounts from the second seat, and comes with a 60-day money-back guarantee.

Stay on DevExtreme if you build data-entry applications with form or popup editing, you are in the DevExpress ecosystem (especially on .NET), and your users do not need to select and copy cell ranges.

Where DevExtreme stops

Four limits, each from DevExpress’s own documentation or our own test:

  • No cell range selection, no clipboard. The selection API offers single, multiple, or none, applied to rows. In the official v26.1.4 demo, dragging across cells selects nothing, Shift+Arrow does nothing, and Ctrl+C leaves the clipboard untouched. The keyboard reference lists no Ctrl+C or Ctrl+V.
  • Hierarchical data and pivoting are separate components. DevExpress documents the DataGrid for “flat data or master-detail scenarios”; trees go to TreeList and pivots to PivotGrid. Same licence, different component, different migration.
  • Export depends on third-party libraries. XLSX needs ExcelJS and FileSaver, PDF needs jsPDF, and “Only XLSX and PDF files are supported out of the box”; CSV is yours to implement.
  • Licence and theming model. A 12-month subscription per developer with no free edition; the console verification message applies to expired as well as unlicensed builds. Themes are precompiled CSS or SCSS whose structure DevExpress describes as internal and subject to change. Accessibility is declared as “WCAG 2.x” without a level, and Windows High Contrast themes are not supported.

The other suites

Kendo UI Grid (Telerik)

Kendo UI is the most direct substitute: a four-framework suite whose grid has locked columns and rows, multi-column headers, detail rows, grouping with aggregates, state persistence, Excel-compatible copy, cut, and paste, a context menu, and native Excel, PDF, and CSV export. Where it clearly beats DevExtreme is accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA declared, a published VPAT, and a documented NVDA and JAWS test matrix. Trade-offs: $799 to $1,299 per developer per year by support tier (KendoReact alone $649 to $1,199), hierarchical data again in a separate TreeList, virtualization only with a fixed row height, and a watermark on perpetual licences if you install a version released after expiry.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you need clipboard support or a signed VPAT inside a suite. Stay on DevExtreme if: you want cheaper renewals and source code in the base price.

Syncfusion Grid

Since July 2026 Syncfusion’s grid can be bought as a standalone Grid SDK (Data Grid, Tree Grid, Pivot Table) for every supported framework including Blazor. The grid is dense: cell selection and autofill, clipboard, native Excel, CSV, and PDF export, lazy-loaded server grouping, and state persistence. Two things to budget for: Syncfusion no longer publishes prices (per developer, minimum one-year subscription, automatic renewals, quote only), and the free community licence has permanent thresholds (under $1M revenue, under $3M lifetime outside capital, five developers, ten employees, not for public sector).

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you qualify for the community licence, or need Blazor and JavaScript from one vendor. Stay on DevExtreme if: you want a published price and a 50% renewal.

The standalone grids

AG Grid

AG Grid is not a suite, and that is the point: $999 per developer buys a perpetual Enterprise licence with one year of updates for JavaScript, React, Angular, and Vue, with everything DevExtreme has in the grid (grouping, aggregation, master-detail, server-side row model), what it keeps in other components (tree data, pivoting), and what it lacks entirely (range selection, clipboard, fill handle, context and column menus). Community (MIT) is a strong free grid but has no clipboard or range selection; those modules are Enterprise.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: the grid is the product and you want it perpetual. Stay on DevExtreme if: you also need the forms, schedulers, and charts in the suite.

MUI X Data Grid

MUI X is React-only and tied to Material UI, which rules it out for DevExtreme shops on Angular or Vue. For a React team, Pro ($299 per developer per year) adds column pinning, unlimited rows, tree data, and master-detail; Premium ($599) adds range selection, clipboard paste, Excel export, grouping, aggregation, and pivoting. Community caps the grid at 100 rows per page (verified in a clean v9.12.0 build), and a Multi App add-on is required for more than one application.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you are a Material UI shop on React. Stay on DevExtreme if: you are multi-framework.

SVAR DataGrid

SVAR DataGrid is MIT for React, Vue, and Svelte, and its free tier includes things DevExtreme sells or lacks: tree data in the grid (verified in the official demo), undo/redo, an AND/OR filter builder, collapsible header groups, inline editing, and an MCP server, with eight feature releases in twelve months. It has no row grouping or aggregation, no Excel export, no range selection or system clipboard, only English and Chinese locale packs, no RTL, and no Angular edition.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you need a free grid with tree data and your app is not international. Stay on DevExtreme if: you need grouped totals, 36 locales, or Angular.

TanStack Table

TanStack Table is headless: sorting, filtering, grouping with aggregation, pagination state, and (in v9) cell selection and spanning, with no markup, styles, editing, virtualization, export, or accessibility layer. Ten official framework adapters on one MIT licence; the opposite of DevExtreme’s batteries-included model.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you were going to restyle every DevExtreme theme anyway. Stay on DevExtreme if: you want editing, validation, and export working this sprint.

CoreUI Data Grid

Our own entry, so judge it against the live demo and the docs. CoreUI Data Grid is a TypeScript grid with row virtualization (100,000-row demo), multi-column sorting, typed filters with faceted set filters and global search, client and server pagination, column pinning and ordering, inline editing with validation and undo/redo, row selection and reordering, multi-range cell selection with clipboard copy (verified in the public demo), Excel export through a built-in .xlsx writer, CSV, printing, save-and-restore state, RTL, a headless API, and an MCP server plus llms.txt. Server-side mode is a single dataProvider function. Every feature is in the one edition.

The structural differences: one $217 per-developer licence is perpetual, includes 12 months of updates, and covers the JavaScript, React, Vue, and Angular editions; there is no licence check at runtime; export needs no third-party library; and theming is CSS custom properties on Bootstrap-compatible markup rather than a precompiled theme.

Honest limits, from our roadmap: the grid is at version 0.4.0 (October 2026, still pre-1.0), and the line-of-business features DevExtreme is strong in are not there yet. Tree data, master-detail, row grouping with aggregation, a summary row, multi-level headers, row pinning, cell spanning, and clipboard paste are planned, not shipped. Editing is inline cell editing only (no batch, form, or popup modes). No pivoting, no context menu, no PDF export, English labels only; screen-reader verification is in progress, so we do not claim a WCAG level.

Choose it over DevExtreme if: you need the core grid across four frameworks on a perpetual licence and do not need grouped totals or form editing today. Stay on DevExtreme if: you rely on batch or popup editing, group summaries, master-detail, or server-side grouping in production.

Worth a look, but not in the table

Handsontable ($999 per developer per year, non-perpetual) is the spreadsheet answer: formulas, fill handle, full clipboard, but no grouping, pivoting, or master-detail. RevoGrid is an MIT web component for five frameworks with range selection in the free core, while pagination, checkbox selection, and undo/redo are Pro. Bryntum Grid and the PrimeNG / PrimeReact / PrimeVue tables are platform decisions more than grid decisions.

Migrating away from DevExtreme

  • Column definitions translate, templates do not. DevExtreme columns (dataField, caption, dataType) map mechanically to CoreUI’s { key, label } plus filterType and formatter, or to an AG Grid colDef. Cell and edit templates are framework-specific everywhere; count them first.
  • Editing modes. No alternative here has an identical set of batch, form, and popup modes. AG Grid and Kendo have full-row editing; CoreUI has inline cell editing with editValidate. Form and popup editing usually moves into your own form components.
  • remoteOperations becomes a data provider. Load options (skip, take, sort, filter) map to CoreUI’s single dataProvider in server-side mode or AG Grid’s server-side row model. Server-side grouping and summaries have no CoreUI equivalent today; this is the biggest migration blocker from DevExtreme.
  • Export gets simpler. Replacing the ExcelJS and FileSaver wiring with a built-in Excel export is one of the few places where migration removes code.
  • State and themes. stateStoring maps to CoreUI’s save-and-restore state; the compiled theme maps to CSS custom properties. Decide whether the grid alone leaves or the whole suite does.
  • Do a two-grid spike first. Rebuild one screen with your heaviest editing mode and largest dataset before touching the rest.

Which one should you pick?

  • Form or popup editing, group summaries, or master-detail, in the DevExpress ecosystem → stay on DevExtreme.
  • Clipboard support and a signed VPAT inside a suite → Kendo UI.
  • One vendor across Blazor and JavaScript, or the community licence → Syncfusion.
  • The grid is the product and you want everything, perpetual → AG Grid Enterprise.
  • Material UI shop on React → MUI X.
  • A free grid with tree data → SVAR.
  • Full design control, you own the maintenance → TanStack Table.
  • Virtualization, filtering, editing, Excel export, and clipboard copy across JavaScript, React, Vue, or Angular, one perpetual licence → CoreUI Data Grid ($217 /dev).

The quickest test: open your DevExtreme config and look for remoteOperations, summary, and masterDetail. If they are there and in use, the alternatives shrink to AG Grid, Kendo, and Syncfusion. If what you have is columns, filterRow, editing.mode: 'cell', and an export button, you are paying suite prices for a table.
